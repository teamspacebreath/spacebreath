Behind the Breath · Creation story

When the breath made us forget the timer.

A subscriber asked for longer holds. Testing them led to an unexpected problem: Jonas kept getting so absorbed in the breath that he forgot he was supposed to be measuring it.

Words by Harry (SpaceBreath)With Jonas and Erich29 September 20265-minute read

This session began with one of the best kinds of prompts: someone who had made our original Power Breathing practice part of their routine asked if we could take the breath holds further.

The comment stayed with us. Revisiting a session because somebody had connected with it felt more meaningful than extending it simply for the sake of making it harder. We wanted the new version to preserve the energy of the original while giving experienced practitioners more time in the stillness that follows each round.

Testing how far to take it

Jonas first asked me to add a full minute to each hold. I prepared a test version, but working out the right timings turned out to be more difficult than expected. Every time Jonas tried to measure how long he could comfortably stay in a hold, he became absorbed in the session and lost track of the calculation.

“I’m so into it, I kind of forget I’m doing breathwork.”Jonas, during the hold tests

Jonas found a simple solution: an audible interval timer that could beep every 15 seconds. During testing he used Beep Every 15 Seconds, which let him stay with his eyes closed and remain inside the experience while still knowing roughly how much time had passed.

That small problem says something lovely about breathwork. You can begin with a practical task in mind, such as counting seconds, and still find yourself drawn so fully into the breath that the calculation disappears.

Finding the final holds

The tests helped us shape four rounds rather than applying the same extension to everything. The first hold was discussed at around 50 seconds and finalised in the edit at 55 seconds. The second became one minute. The final two move into much longer holds after the exhale.

  • Round 0155-second hold after the inhale
  • Round 0260-second hold after the inhale
  • Round 032-minute 45-second hold after the exhale
  • Round 043-minute 15-second hold after the exhale

These times belong to the guided structure, but they are not goals. Anyone practising should breathe as soon as their body asks for air. The longer holds also mean this is an advanced session for people who already know how they respond to Power Breathing and retention.

Returning to an early track

The original music came from our fourth SpaceBreath session. I did not want to rewrite it or lose the character people already knew, but our production process has changed since then. This was a chance to remaster the track using what I have learnt across the sessions that followed.

I reduced the bass, eased back the drums and percussion, and softened the kick so it would sit more gently in the mix. Those changes made the session easier on the ears and allowed details that had previously been crowded to come through more clearly. The score still moves with its original 111 BPM pulse, but the balance now feels calmer and more spacious.

Making the risk visible

Because the final holds became significantly longer, the safety information needed to be difficult to miss. We asked Erich to create a clear banner directing viewers to check the description before starting.

Long breath holds are not suitable for everyone. Clear guidance cannot remove every risk, but it can help people make an informed decision, choose a safe setting and understand that the timer is a guide rather than a target. That responsibility had to be part of the session itself.

It was a pleasure to return to an early piece of music and turn a thoughtful subscriber request into a new practice. We hope the result feels both more expansive and more considered.
