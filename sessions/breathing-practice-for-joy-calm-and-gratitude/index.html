Session overview

At a glance

This eight-minute practice uses two rounds at 70 BPM. Each round combines 20 regular breaths with 24 faster connected breaths, followed by an exhale hold of one minute in round one and one minute 15 seconds in round two.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

A short emotional reset for people already familiar with active guided breathing and looking for a lighter internal state.

Time

For days when you want a complete guided practice without committing to a longer journey.

Intention

Experience

Familiar with active breathwork

Guided session

Breathe with us

Sit or lie down somewhere safe and comfortable. Keep the breath within a range that feels manageable, and return to your natural breathing whenever you need to.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have a cardiovascular condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to your natural breathing and rest.

The intention

Remember what is already there

True happiness does not always need to be found. Sometimes we simply need a way back to it.

When doubt, tension or difficult emotions appear, it is easy to react before we have had time to notice what is happening. Conscious breathing can create a pause: a few minutes to listen, reset and choose how we meet the moment.

This short practice is designed as an emotional reset for people already familiar with active guided breathwork. The pace builds through each round, then gives way to stillness so calm, clarity and gratitude have room to surface.

Welcome and intention

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today is about remembering true happiness, the happiness that comes from within and is always with us. We just sometimes forget about it.

I have found that breathwork is a powerful way to activate that beautiful state that is always there.

We will do two rounds in total, breathing at two different paces, with breath holds of about one minute at the end of each round on the exhale.

All right. Make yourself comfortable, sitting upright or lying down, and we will begin in three, two, one.

Round one

Feel into these inhales through your nose, how the air enters, and your exhale through your mouth. Just connect with your body here.

Two more before we go faster. Just a few more from here.

And now take a deep inhale. Fully let go. We stay here for one minute.

Feel into the space around you and inside you. Notice the peace. There is nothing to be worried about.

Three, two, one. Inhale and hold here for a moment. Just feel and listen to the sounds. And exhale.

Round two

All right, we will begin with our second round in three, two, one.

Be very present with every inhale. Feel how you are getting, step by step, closer to your true self, to true happiness.

Two more and we will pick up the pace. Stay very aware, very alert. Just be here.

Okay, just a few more. And now take a deep inhale. Fully let go. We stay here for one minute and 15 seconds.

Fully relax into this breath hold. Let go of any tension in your shoulders, neck or jaw. Smile to yourself. Feel. Just feel into yourself.

Three, two, one. Inhale and hold here for a moment. And release.

Closing

Slowly return to your natural breathing through your nose. Stay as long as you like here in this beautiful state.

I hope you enjoyed the session. Enjoy the rest of your day, and see you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Three practical answers about the session length, breathing pattern and its relationship to other active practices.

How long is this breathing practice?

The complete guided practice lasts approximately eight minutes and contains two active breathing rounds.

How many breaths are in each round?

Each round combines 20 regular breaths and 24 faster connected breaths, followed by an exhale hold and a short recovery breath.

Is this the Wim Hof breathing method?

No. It shares elements found in active breathwork, but it follows SpaceBreath’s own two-round structure, timing, guidance and original 70 BPM score.
