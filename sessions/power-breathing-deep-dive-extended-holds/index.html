Session overview

At a glance

This is an advanced 28-minute guided Power Breathing practice in four rounds. The first two rounds use progressively faster, deeper breathing and finish with holds after the inhale. The final two slow the pace and finish with longer holds after the exhale, guided up to 2 minutes 45 seconds and 3 minutes 15 seconds.

The hold times are guides, never targets. Breathe earlier whenever you need to.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

For people already familiar with Power Breathing and breath retention who want a longer, more demanding practice. If you are new to this style, begin with the original Power Breathing session and take time to learn its rhythm first.

Safety and contraindications

This is not a beginner session. Rapid breathing and long holds can cause tingling, light-headedness or dizziness and, in rare cases, loss of consciousness. The hold times are guides, not targets. Never force a hold. If you feel unwell, stop and return to normal breathing.

Practise seated or lying down in a safe place. Never practise in or near water, while driving, standing, operating machinery or anywhere a loss of consciousness could injure you or someone else. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy. If you have a cardiovascular condition or another serious medical condition, seek advice from a qualified healthcare professional before practising. This session is for general wellbeing and education, not medical advice.

Stop if you feel dizzy or unwell. Breathe before the hold timer ends whenever your body asks for air.

How the breathing works

Build the energy.
Give it space.

Start each round with controlled belly-to-chest breathing and a relaxed exhale. The first two rounds rise through faster paces and end with holds after a full inhale. Rounds three and four move into a slower cool-down and longer holds after the exhale. Return to natural breathing between rounds whenever needed.

The intention

An extended journey shaped by your comments

You asked for longer holds in the original Power Breathing session. This version gives the breathing rounds more room and makes the final exhale holds the centre of the cool-down.

It is a challenging practice, and the longest timer is not a goal to achieve. Stay responsive to how you feel and skip or shorten any hold. You can also pause before moving into another round.

This is an independent SpaceBreath session inspired by a Wim Hof-style breathing pattern, not an official Wim Hof Method exercise.

Session transcript

Read the guidance

A lightly edited selection of the spoken guidance. Repeated breathing cues and music-only passages are condensed.

Open transcriptEnglish · around 28 minutes

Welcome and safety

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. In today’s journey, we’re doing Wim Hof-style power breathing with extended breath holds. Four rounds in total, breathing at different paces. The first half is the more activating part, with holds after the inhale. The second half is a cool-down with longer holds after the exhale.

Breathe into your tummy first, then draw the breath up into your chest. As this is an intense session, make sure you’re in a safe environment. I recommend lying down. Make yourself comfortable. We’ll start with slow breaths, tummy to chest.

Round one: activating breath

Slow and conscious breathing. Fill your tummy first and draw the breath up into your chest. Use this slow breathing to connect with your body. Notice the movement of each inhale and exhale.

Stay close to your breath. Feel your inhales like a wave coming in: your tummy rises, then your chest, followed by a soft exhale. Stay present as we pick up the pace. Stay in the rhythm: tummy, chest, out.

Now take a deep inhale and hold at the top. Softly engage your body, beginning with your pelvic floor, abdomen and chest. Feel the energy building. Then exhale.

Round two: build

We’re heading straight into the second round. Stay alert and aware of each breath. Notice the air entering and your tummy and chest rising. As the pattern gets faster, keep your inhales strong and your exhales soft.

Take a deep inhale and hold after the inhale. Gently engage your upper body and feel into the hold. Then fully exhale.

Round three: cool down and longer exhale hold

Now we’re entering our cool-down phase. We’ll take some deep, slow breaths together before the first long hold. Inhale deeply, without forcing the exhale.

After the last deep inhale, fully let go. This exhale hold is guided for two minutes and 45 seconds. Relax your face, jaw, neck and shoulders. When the countdown ends, inhale, pause briefly and release.

Round four: final power round

We’re heading into our final round. It can be challenging, so return to slow, conscious breathing first. Feel your tummy and chest expand. As the pace rises, stay aware of your breath and make each inhale deep without forcing it.

After one last deep inhale, fully let go. The final exhale hold is guided for three minutes and 15 seconds. Notice any sensations or feelings and let the body relax. When the countdown ends, inhale, pause briefly and fully release.

Closing

Continue with your natural breathing through your nose. Take all the time you need. Thank you for being part of this intense session today. I hope you enjoyed it. See you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

What to know about the holds and how this version compares with the original.

Is this suitable for beginners?

No. This advanced session includes faster breathing and holds guided up to 3 minutes 15 seconds. Start with the original or a beginner session if you are new to breath retention.

Which holds are after the inhale?

Rounds one and two finish with inhale holds of 55 seconds and one minute. Rounds three and four finish after the exhale, guided for 2 minutes 45 seconds and 3 minutes 15 seconds.

Must I finish the timer?

No. The times are guides, never targets. Breathe as soon as you need to, and stop if you feel unwell.
