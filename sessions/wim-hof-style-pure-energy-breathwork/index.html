Session overview

At a glance

This 15-minute session contains two contrasting rounds. The first moves from slow breathing into two faster stages and ends with a 45-second inhale hold. The second begins naturally, slows into deep breaths and ends with a 90-second exhale hold.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

An energising practice for people familiar with active breathing who want a focused session with a gradual cool-down rather than repeated fast rounds.

Guided session

Breathe with us

Sit or lie down somewhere safe and comfortable. Keep the breath within a manageable range and return to natural breathing whenever needed.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for education and general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, a cardiovascular condition or a serious respiratory condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

Read the full safety guide →

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to natural breathing and rest.

The intention

Intensity followed by a deliberate return

Rather than repeating the same active round, this practice builds once and then gives equal importance to slowing down.

The first round focuses on deep inhales and soft releases as the pace increases. The inhale hold adds gentle full-body engagement before the practice changes direction.

The second round gradually reduces the pace and ends with quiet stillness. This is a SpaceBreath practice inspired by Wim Hof-style power breathing, not an official Wim Hof Method exercise or a medical treatment.

Welcome and technique

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today we are moving through a short energising practice inspired by Wim Hof’s power breathing.

There are two rounds: an activating first round with different breathing rhythms and a short inhale hold, followed by a cool-down round with calmer breaths and a final exhale hold of about one minute 30 seconds.

You can lie down or sit upright. I recommend breathing in and out through the mouth, but you can switch to nasal breathing whenever that feels better.

Round one

Begin with conscious deep breaths as a gentle warm-up. Inhale into the tummy first, then allow the breath to rise into the chest. After two more breaths, increase the pace.

Find the rhythm: tummy, chest, soft release. Stay present with each breath and notice how the body moves. Increase the pace one final time, keeping the focus on deep inhales followed by a soft release rather than a full exhale.

Take a strong, full inhale and hold. Activate the body with a comfortable squeeze from the legs through the pelvic floor, abdomen and chest to the top of the head. Three, two, one. Fully release.

Round two

Move into the final cool-down round with regular easy breaths. Inhale deeply and steadily, then exhale gently. Notice the sensations in the body and relax a little more with every breath.

Slow the breathing and take several deep, unhurried breaths. For the final breath, inhale as deeply as is comfortable, then fully release. Stay after the exhale for one minute 30 seconds. Notice and soften tension in the face, neck or jaw.

Closing

Three, two, one. Take a deep inhale and hold briefly, perhaps adding one last gentle squeeze, then fully let go.

Return slowly to the space around you with small movements in the fingers and toes. Let the breath return to its natural rhythm through the nose. Thank you for sharing the journey. See you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Practical answers about the pattern, structure and how to approach the session.

Is this an official Wim Hof Method exercise?

No. It is a SpaceBreath practice inspired by a similar power-breathing style.

How are the two rounds different?

The first round increases in speed and ends with a 45-second inhale hold. The second gradually slows and ends with a 90-second exhale hold.

Can I breathe through my nose?

Yes. Mouth breathing is suggested for the active practice, but you can use nasal breathing whenever it feels more appropriate.
