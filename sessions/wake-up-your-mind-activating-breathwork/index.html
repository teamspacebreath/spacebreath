Session overview

At a glance

This eight-minute activating practice contains three rounds of strong mouth breathing. Each round uses powerful inhales and short, relaxed exhales before a one-minute hold after the exhale.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

A short practice for experienced breathwork participants who want an energising reset and are comfortable with stronger breathing and exhale holds.

Time

Eight focused minutes

For mornings, work breaks or moments when you want a concise practice.

Intention

Energy and mental clarity

Designed to help you feel more awake, present and connected with your body.

Experience

Active breathing

Best suited to people already comfortable with strong mouth breathing and breath holds.

Guided session

Breathe with us

Sit or lie down somewhere safe and comfortable. Keep the breath within a manageable range and return to natural breathing whenever needed.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for education and general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, a cardiovascular condition or a serious respiratory condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to natural breathing and rest.

The intention

Strong breath, followed by stillness

This practice uses contrast: active breathing to build intensity, then quiet holds to create space.

The inhale is deliberately strong, beginning in the belly and rising through the chest. The exhale is not forced; it is a short release before the next inhale.

The practice is presented as an energising experience rather than a medical treatment. Keep the breath within a controllable range and stop if the intensity no longer feels appropriate.

Welcome and technique

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. In today’s journey, we are going to do strong activation breathwork. This practice is built to bring fresh energy into your body and mind.

The inhale should be very strong, like when you come up from a dive, and the exhale is just a release. Try to lift your rib cage with every inhale. Breathe into your tummy first to create more space and go as deep as feels manageable.

I recommend mouth breathing for this one. You can sit upright or lie down, whatever feels best for you. Let’s get started in three, two, one.

Round one

Draw every inhale up strongly. Breathe deeply into your tummy, rising into your chest. Make the inhales explosive.

Now take a deep inhale. Fully let go. We stay here for one minute. Go into deep relaxation. Let go of any tension in your shoulders, neck, face, hands and feet.

Three, two, one. Inhale and hold. Gently engage your pelvic floor and abdominals, and exhale.

Rounds two and three

Let’s continue in three, two, one. Move your body with each breath. Bring your chest up to go deeper and create more space.

Strong inhale. Fully let go. We stay here for one minute. Go into full relaxation and enjoy the stillness.

Our final round starts in three, two, one. Go deep in this last round. Inhale explosively, with the chest lifting up.

Inhale and hold. Fully exhale. We stay here for one minute. Let go of any tension still within you and enjoy this last long breath hold.

Closing

Three, two, one. Deep inhale. Hold here for a moment, and exhale.

Continue breathing softly in and out through your nose. Feel into your body and the fresh energy you have recharged. Do not forget to share it with the world out there. See you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Practical answers about the pattern, structure and how to approach the session.

How long is this activating breathwork session?

The complete guided session lasts eight minutes and contains three active breathing rounds.

How should the breathing feel?

The inhale is strong and deliberate, while the exhale is a short, relaxed release. The practice should remain controlled rather than strained.

How long are the breath holds?

Each of the three rounds finishes with an exhale hold of approximately one minute.
