Session overview

At a glance

This 21-minute practice contains three rounds of fast connected breathing. Each round ends with a 30-second inhale hold and an exhale hold that increases from one minute to two minutes.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

Guided session

Breathe with us

Sit or lie down somewhere safe and comfortable. Keep the breath within a manageable range and return to natural breathing whenever needed.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for education and general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, a cardiovascular condition or a serious respiratory condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to natural breathing and rest.

The intention

A shorter psychedelic-inspired journey

The session uses sustained rhythmic breathing followed by quiet holds as an invitation to observe the body and mind with curiosity.

The word “psychedelic” describes the breathing style and subjective qualities that some participants may notice. It does not establish or guarantee the release of DMT, pineal-gland activation or a medical effect.

Approach the practice without expectations. Stay within a controllable range, pause if needed and return to natural nasal breathing whenever the intensity becomes uncomfortable.

Welcome and technique

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today we are moving through a guided psychedelic breathing session. The breathing pace is fast from the beginning. The technique is simple: tummy, chest, release, without a full exhale.

The active phases are longer, so you may notice tingling in the hands or face. If it feels like too much, pause and return to normal nasal breathing.

We complete three rounds. Each ends with a short inhale hold, engaging gently from the pelvic floor through the abdomen and chest, followed by a longer hold after the exhale. You can sit or lie down and breathe through the mouth, or in through the nose and out through the mouth.

Round one

Begin with a few slow, deep breaths to connect with the body. Feel the air fill the tummy, rise into the chest and perhaps reach towards the head.

Move into the fast tummy, chest, release rhythm. Keep the flow steady without a gap or full exhale. If the mind wanders, notice it and return to the breath.

Take a deep inhale and hold. Engage gently through the body, then fully exhale and stay for one minute. Release tension in the shoulders and face.

Rounds two and three

For round two, return to the flow and notice every detail of the breath. Focus on strong inhales. Take a full inhale and hold, engage gently, then exhale completely and stay for one minute 30 seconds. Relax the jaw and neck.

In the final round, stay fully present. Notice sensations without panic and reduce the pace if needed. Deeply inhale, hold briefly and then release fully. Stay after the exhale for two minutes and explore what you feel with curiosity.

Closing

Three, two, one. Deep inhale, hold and add one final gentle squeeze. Exhale completely.

Return slowly to normal conscious breathing through the nose. Notice the contact between the body and the ground, move the fingers and toes, and stretch if that feels good. Thank you for joining us. See you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Practical answers about the pattern, structure and how to approach the session.

Does this session release DMT?

The original video title uses that phrase, but breathwork-induced DMT release has not been established here. The page describes the practice without presenting that claim as fact.

How long are the breath holds?

Every round has an inhale hold of about 30 seconds. The exhale holds last one minute, one minute 30 seconds and two minutes.

Is this suitable for beginners?

It is relatively intense. Beginners may prefer a shorter or gentler session and should never force the breathing or holds.
