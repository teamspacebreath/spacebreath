Session overview

At a glance

This 15-minute evening practice uses a four-second inhale through the nose and a six-second exhale through gently pursed lips. Three rounds end with relaxed exhale holds of 30, 45 and 60 seconds.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

A gentle practice for unwinding in the evening, preparing for meditation or creating a softer transition towards sleep.

Time

Fifteen minutes

Three unhurried rounds with a quiet closing.

Intention

Unwind and settle

For releasing the momentum of the day and returning attention to the body.

Experience

Gentle pacing

Slow breathing with optional, gradually longer exhale holds.

Guided session

Breathe with us

Sit or lie down somewhere safe and comfortable. Keep the breath within a manageable range and return to natural breathing whenever needed.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for education and general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, a cardiovascular condition or a serious respiratory condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

Read the full safety guide →

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to natural breathing and rest.

The intention

A simple rhythm for leaving the day behind

The slightly longer exhale gives attention a calm, repeatable pattern without requiring fast or forceful breathing.

The original description refers to the parasympathetic nervous system and feelings of safety. These describe the purpose of the session rather than a guaranteed medical effect.

Keep every breath comfortable. If the count or hold feels strained, shorten it or return to natural breathing. The practice can be done lying down if you are preparing for sleep.

Open transcriptEnglish · 15 minutes

Welcome and technique

Good evening, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today we are doing a short evening practice to calm the body and mind and support a restful night.

Begin with a gentle four-second inhale through the nose, followed by a slow six-second exhale through the mouth. As you exhale, softly purse the lips as though blowing through a straw.

We move through three rounds and finish each with a relaxed hold after the exhale, beginning at 30 seconds and gradually extending to one minute. Lie down if you are preparing for sleep, or sit if that feels better.

Round one

Make yourself comfortable and perhaps use a blanket. Begin with the repeated four-second inhale and six-second exhale. Let the inhale start in the tummy and allow it to rise naturally into the chest.

Notice every breath: the air at the nose, the feeling of filling from within and the gentle release through the lips. After the final exhale, stay for 30 seconds. Deeply inhale, hold softly for a moment and release.

Rounds two and three

For round two, continue focusing on deep inhales into the tummy and let the chest rise naturally. Whenever the mind drifts, return to the details of each breath. After the final exhale, rest for 45 seconds and simply observe how you feel.

In the final round, stay close to the inhale and let go of anything that no longer serves you with every exhale. After the deepest comfortable inhale, fully release and stay for one minute. Soften the shoulders, neck and jaw.

Closing

Deeply inhale, hold for a moment, observe and fully let go. Slowly return to the space around you with small movements in the fingers and toes, keeping the calmness in the body.

Continue gentle natural breathing through the nose. Thank you for joining us tonight. I hope you enjoyed the session and that it supports a restful night. See you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Practical answers about the pattern, structure and how to approach the session.

What is the breathing pattern?

Inhale gently through the nose for four seconds, then exhale through softly pursed lips for six seconds.

How long are the breath holds?

The holds after the exhale last up to 30 seconds, 45 seconds and one minute.

Can I practise this in bed?

Yes, if you are preparing for sleep. Make sure your position is comfortable and return to natural breathing if any part feels strained.
