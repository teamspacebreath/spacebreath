Session overview

At a glance

This eight-minute evening session guides a continuous 4-7-8 breathing pattern: inhale for four seconds, hold for seven and exhale slowly for eight. The guided practice begins at 01:12 and continues for just over five minutes.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

A slow evening practice for people who want a simple counted rhythm before bed or while unwinding after the day.

Time

Eight minutes

A short introduction, five-minute practice and quiet closing.

Intention

Evening relaxation

Designed to support a gradual transition towards rest.

Experience

Gentle and counted

No fast breathing or extended breath retention is used.

Guided session

Breathe with us

Sit or lie down somewhere safe and comfortable. Keep the breath within a manageable range and return to natural breathing whenever needed.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for education and general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, a cardiovascular condition or a serious respiratory condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to natural breathing and rest.

The breathing pattern

Four in. Seven hold.
Eight out.

Follow the three-part count without straining. The long exhale should remain smooth, and the seven-second pause can be shortened whenever needed.

The intention

A predictable rhythm for the end of the day

The repeated count gives attention a simple structure while the music avoids sudden changes or sharp sounds.

The original video title says “Fall Asleep in 5 Minutes”, but sleep cannot be guaranteed. This page presents the exercise as an evening relaxation practice that may help some people settle.

If the seven-second hold or eight-second exhale feels difficult, shorten the count. Comfortable breathing is more important than matching every number.

Session transcript

Read the guidance

A reviewed transcript of the spoken introduction and closing. Repeated 4-7-8 counting is described rather than duplicated cycle by cycle.

Open transcriptEnglish · 8 minutes

Introduction

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today’s practice is short and uses the 4-7-8 breathing method, a technique often used as an evening relaxation exercise.

Gently place the tongue on the roof of the mouth just behind the teeth. Inhale through the nose for four counts, hold for seven and exhale through the mouth for eight, gently pursing the lips.

We stay with the pattern for about five minutes. Make yourself comfortable, lying down or sitting upright. When you are ready, we begin gently through the nose and smoothly out through the mouth.

Guided 4-7-8 breathing

Jonas guides repeated cycles of a four-count inhale, seven-count hold and eight-count exhale. The pace remains slow and consistent throughout the central practice.

The final cycle follows the same count before the guidance invites you to return to your own natural rhythm.

Closing

Return to your natural breath, breathing softly through the nose. You can return to this practice when the mind feels busy or overactive. I hope you feel relaxed and calm. See you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Practical answers about the pattern, structure and how to approach the session.

What is 4-7-8 breathing?

It is a counted pattern of inhaling for four seconds, holding for seven and exhaling for eight.

Will this make me fall asleep in five minutes?

Sleep cannot be guaranteed. The session is designed as a gentle evening relaxation practice that may make it easier to settle.

What if I cannot hold for seven seconds?

Shorten the hold or return to natural breathing. The pattern should feel calm and manageable, never strained.
