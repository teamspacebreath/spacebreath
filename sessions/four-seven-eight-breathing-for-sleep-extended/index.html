Session overview

At a glance

The extended version of our eight-minute 4-7-8 session. Jonas guides the pattern from 00:50 for ten minutes, inhaling through the nose and exhaling through the mouth. At 10:50 the guidance ends and the music carries on, so you can stop counting and simply drift.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

For anyone who wants a longer wind-down at bedtime, or who has tried the shorter version and wants more time to settle into the rhythm.

Time

Fourteen minutes

Ten minutes of guided breathing, then quiet time to rest.

Intention

Falling asleep

Designed to help you let go of the day and drift off.

Experience

Gentle and counted

No fast breathing. Only a short, comfortable hold.

Guided session

Breathe with us

Lie down in bed or somewhere safe and comfortable. Breathe in through the nose and out through the mouth, and let the count guide you.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for education and general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, a cardiovascular condition or a serious respiratory condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

Read the full safety guide →

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to natural breathing and rest. Shorten the hold whenever you need to.

The breathing pattern

Four in. Seven hold.
Eight out.

The out-breath is twice as long as the in-breath, which gently slows the body down. Follow the count without straining, and shorten the hold whenever you need to.

The intention

Time to stop counting

Ten minutes gives the rhythm time to settle, so you can stop following the numbers and simply drift.

The 4-7-8 technique was popularised by Dr Andrew Weil. Jonas counts every phase at first, then the guidance thins out to a gentle “hold” so you can follow the pattern on your own. Midway through: “Stay very present. Always come back to your breathing.”

After the last breath, around four minutes of music remain. If you are still awake, return to your natural breathing and rest. This is a wellbeing practice and not a treatment for insomnia.

About the music

A softer score that rises
and falls like a wave.

Harry reworked the music from our original 8-minute 4-7-8 session for this longer practice. He pitched the key up for a more relaxing feel, took out the drums and bass hits, and replaced them with binaural bass. Many of the original sounds are softened, with subtle white noise underneath. New layers give the piece more variation: an Armenian duduk, subtle strings, soft keys and more chord changes that open the music up. In the middle it rises and then falls again, swelling and softening like a wave, to help you drift off.

Introduction

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today’s journey is all about slowing down body, mind and breath to prepare yourself for a restful night’s sleep.

We will be doing ten minutes of the 4-7-8 breathing technique: a gentle four-second inhale, a seven-second hold and an eight-second slow exhale. It’s very simple and effective. Just follow my guidance.

All right, then please make yourself comfortable. We will inhale through the nose and exhale through the mouth. Let’s begin in three, two, one.

Guided breathing

In, two, three, four. Hold, two, three, four, five, six, seven. Out, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight. Jonas counts the first few minutes in full, then gives a softer “hold” cue so you can follow the rhythm on your own.

“Stay very present. Always come back to your breathing.”

Closing

This is the last breath of today’s session. Thank you for being here. Enjoy the rest of your evening, and see you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Practical answers about the pattern, the hold and how to approach the session.

How is this different from the 8-minute version?

Same technique, longer practice. The guided breathing runs for ten minutes instead of five, followed by four minutes of music to help you drift off.

What if the seven-second hold feels too long?

Shorten it. Keep the pattern comfortable, with the exhale longer than the inhale, and return to natural breathing whenever you need to.

Will this help me fall asleep?

Many people use 4-7-8 breathing to wind down at bedtime, but it isn’t a medical treatment and results vary. If sleep problems continue, speak to a healthcare professional.
