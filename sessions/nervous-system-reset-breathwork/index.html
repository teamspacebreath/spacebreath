Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for education and general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, a cardiovascular condition or a serious respiratory condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

Read the full safety guide →

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to natural breathing and rest.

The intention

Controlled challenge followed by deep rest

The practice is inspired by hormetic breathwork, using manageable active phases followed by deliberate relaxation.

The published video describes vagus nerve stimulation and nervous system regulation. On this page, those phrases describe the intention of the session rather than a guaranteed medical effect. Evidence and individual responses vary.

Use the practice as general wellbeing content, not treatment for anxiety. If you have persistent symptoms or a relevant medical condition, consult an appropriate healthcare professional.

About the music

A minimalist 130 BPM ambient pulse.

The original music uses a steady 4/4 structure at 130 BPM. Sampled strings, choir pads, deep sub-bass, plucked strings, a processed meditation bell and a muted snare made from a recorded door-handle knock grow in complexity through the rounds.

Welcome and technique

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today’s practice is designed as a full nervous system reset. We begin with slow, deep inhales, gradually building the intensity with each round. Each round finishes with a hold after the exhale, starting at one minute and extending to two minutes.

You can practise sitting upright or lying down. Begin by inhaling through the nose and exhaling through the mouth. Later, you may choose to breathe only through the mouth to go deeper.

Start with deep inhales into the tummy and then the chest. Bring your awareness into the practice, feel how the body moves and gently release tension with every exhale.

Round one

Stay present. Notice the air entering through the nostrils and softly leaving through the lips. Take a very deep inhale, filling the tummy and chest, then fully let go.

Stay after the exhale for one minute. Notice where the shoulders, neck and face can relax further. Three, two, one. Deep inhale, hold for a moment and fully release.

Rounds two and three

For round two, slightly increase the pace while keeping the same pattern: deeply into the tummy, up into the chest, then a soft exhale. Keep the inhale and exhale connected without a gap.

Fully inhale and let go. Stay after the exhale for one minute 30 seconds. Be present in the stillness and allow the body to relax.

For the final round, you may switch to breathing in and out through the mouth. Accelerate again, with deep inhales and quick releasing exhales. Take the deepest comfortable inhale of the session, let go and stay after the exhale for two minutes. Soften the shoulders and jaw.

Closing

Three, two, one. Deep inhale, hold briefly and fully release.

Slowly return to the space around you. Move the fingers and toes gently and let the breath flow naturally through the nose. Thank you for joining today’s session. Take the positive energy with you and share it with the world. See you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Practical answers about the pattern, structure and how to approach the session.

Why does the original title say 11 minutes?

The central three-round practice lasts approximately 11 minutes. The complete video, including the introduction and closing, is just under 15 minutes.

Does this medically reset the nervous system?

No guaranteed medical outcome is claimed. It is a guided general wellbeing practice intended to create contrast between active breathing and rest.

How long are the holds?

The holds after the exhale last up to one minute, one minute 30 seconds and two minutes.
