Session overview

At a glance

This seven-minute practice uses a triple-inhale pattern through two rounds. Each breath moves into the belly, chest and upper chest before a relaxed exhale. The rounds finish with exhale holds of one minute and one minute 15 seconds.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

A concise practice for people familiar with active breathwork who want to pause mental noise and return to a more focused internal state.

Time

A complete practice that fits into a short break or morning routine.

Intention

Experience

Two active rounds

Guided session

Breathe with us

Sit or lie down somewhere safe and comfortable. Keep the breath within a manageable range and return to natural breathing whenever needed.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for education and general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, a cardiovascular condition or a serious respiratory condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to natural breathing and rest.

The intention

A physical anchor for a busy mind

The repeated three-part inhale gives your attention one clear pattern to follow.

Each inhale moves through the belly, chest and upper chest before the exhale is allowed to fall away naturally. This creates a simple physical sequence that can hold attention when the mind feels crowded.

The holds provide a quieter contrast after each active round. There is no need to compete with the timer; return to natural breathing whenever your body asks you to.

Welcome and technique

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today we are diving into a breathwork journey for mental clarity.

It builds on a triple inhale. Think of it as breathing into your tummy first, then into the chest, and then taking the final part up into your head.

I like breathing in and out through the mouth with this one, but nose breathing or a combination works too. Make yourself comfortable, sitting or lying down. We begin in three, two, one.

Round one

Connect to the pattern. Bring the inhales up and let the exhales be a soft release.

The last two from here. Now take a deep inhale. Fully let go. We stay here for one minute.

Three, two, one. Inhale and hold. And exhale.

Round two

Let’s get straight into our final round, starting in three, two, one. Keep the inhales strong. Go a little deeper with every breath.

The last three from here. Now take a deep, strong inhale. Fully let go. We stay here for one minute and 15 seconds.

Shift your awareness inside. Scan through every part of your body and feel the stillness that has always been there.

Closing

Three, two, one. Deep inhale and hold. Fully exhale.

Return to your natural breathing rhythm, softly in and out through the nose, aware of every breath. Thank you for being part of this journey. Enjoy the rest of your day, and see you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Practical answers about the pattern, structure and how to approach the session.

What is a triple inhale?

It is one inhale taken in three connected stages: first into the belly, then the chest, then the upper chest.

How many rounds are in this session?

The practice contains two active rounds followed by exhale holds of one minute and one minute 15 seconds.

Can I breathe through my nose?

The guidance favours mouth breathing, but nasal breathing or a comfortable combination can be used if that feels more appropriate.
