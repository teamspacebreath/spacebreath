Session overview

At a glance

This 20-minute practice uses three rounds of a four-second inhale through the nose and an eight-second exhale through gently pursed lips. Each round closes with a relaxed exhale hold, increasing from 45 seconds to one minute 15 seconds.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

A slower practice for people who want to step away from overthinking, settle into the body and use a longer exhale as a steady point of attention.

Time

Experience

Guided session

Breathe with us

Sit or lie down somewhere safe and comfortable. Keep the breath within a manageable range and return to natural breathing whenever needed.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for education and general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, a cardiovascular condition or a serious respiratory condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to natural breathing and rest.

The intention

Give the mind one slow rhythm to follow

The longer exhale creates a spacious, repeatable pattern that can help attention move away from mental noise and towards the body.

The inhale lasts four seconds and the exhale lasts eight. Keep the breath quiet and comfortable rather than trying to empty every last amount of air.

Although the session is described as somatic and grounding, it is a general wellbeing practice rather than treatment for anxiety or another health condition.

Open transcriptEnglish · 20 minutes

Welcome and technique

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today’s session is very meditative, a tool for different moments in life. Maybe you want to relax, your mind feels busy, or you need something grounding.

We will move through three rounds using the same breathing pattern: a four-second inhale through the nose and a long eight-second exhale through the mouth. For the exhale, gently purse your lips as if you are blowing through a straw.

At the end of each round, we hold after the exhale. We begin with 45 seconds and gradually increase to one minute 15 seconds in the final round. Always listen to your body. This is not about pushing, but staying connected to yourself.

Present-moment focus

Notice how the air enters through the nose, how the tummy and chest rise, and how the air leaves through the lips. These small details can anchor you in the present moment.

If thoughts or emotions arise, simply observe them. You do not need to change anything. Guide your attention back to the sensation of breathing and remember that everything that arises will eventually pass.

Three rounds

Round one follows the repeated four-second inhale and eight-second exhale, then rests after the final exhale for 45 seconds. Deeply inhale, hold briefly and fully release.

In round two, connect the breath with the counting so the inhale and exhale flow smoothly into each other. Notice the air at the nostrils and the movement of the tummy and chest. After the final exhale, rest for one minute and soften the shoulders, neck and face.

The final round repeats the same pattern. Whatever appears in the mind, return gently to the small details of the breath. After the last exhale, stay for one minute 15 seconds.

Closing

Deeply inhale, hold for a moment and fully release. Take another full inhale through the nose and let go with a sigh.

Continue with natural breathing through the nose and remain in this meditative state for as long as you like. I hope the session supported you and that you can meet what life brings with a little more calm and ease. See you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Practical answers about the pattern, structure and how to approach the session.

What is the breathing pattern?

Inhale through the nose for four seconds, then exhale gently through pursed lips for eight seconds.

How long are the breath holds?

The three holds after the exhale last up to 45 seconds, one minute and one minute 15 seconds.

Is this treatment for anxiety?

No. It is a general wellbeing and self-exploration practice, not medical treatment. Seek professional support for persistent or severe symptoms.
