Session overview

At a glance

This 13-minute session contains three rounds of faster-paced breathing. Each round finishes with a hold after the inhale while gently engaging the body, followed by a relaxed hold after the exhale. The holds increase with every round.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

An active practice for people familiar with breathwork who want an energising morning session or a focused reset before movement.

Guided session

Breathe with us

Sit or lie down somewhere safe and comfortable. Keep the breath within a manageable range and return to natural breathing whenever needed.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for education and general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, a cardiovascular condition or a serious respiratory condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

Read the full safety guide →

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to natural breathing and rest.

The intention

Energy that moves through the whole body

The practice combines active breathing, gentle muscular engagement and rest to create a clear contrast between effort and release.

During each inhale hold, the guidance invites you to engage from the pelvic floor through the abdomen and upwards through the body. Treat this as a light, controlled action rather than a forceful strain.

The session is presented as an energising wellbeing practice, not a medical treatment or guaranteed performance aid. Reduce the pace or return to natural breathing whenever needed.

Welcome and technique

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today we are diving into three rounds of energising breathwork. We will breathe at a fast pace, finishing each round with changing breath holds on the inhale and the exhale.

During the inhale holds, I will ask you to engage your upper body from the pelvic floor through the abdominals and heart area, all the way to your head. Imagine the pelvic area as a source of energy that you spread through the body by flexing the muscles. This is followed by deep relaxation during the hold after the exhale.

I recommend breathing in and out through the mouth, although you can use the nose if that feels more comfortable. You can practise lying down or sitting upright. Follow the fast pattern, with deep inhales and short exhales.

Round one

Find your rhythm and make the inhale slightly explosive. With every exhale, bring relaxation into the body.

Take a deep inhale and hold. Flex the whole body through the pelvic floor, core, arms and chest. Three, two, one. Fully let go and hold after the exhale. Bring complete relaxation into the body.

Three, two, one. Deep inhale and hold for a moment. Flex the whole body again, then fully release.

Rounds two and three

Let’s go straight into round two. Keep the pattern, with strong inhales and short but deeply relaxing exhales, almost like a quiet sigh.

Take a full inhale and hold at the top. Engage the whole body from bottom to top. Release completely and stay here for one minute. Notice where you can soften further, perhaps in the shoulders, neck or face.

For the final round, stay fully present and bring your attention to every detail of the breath. Take a very deep inhale and hold at the top. Give the body the strongest comfortable squeeze of the session. Fully let go and stay after the exhale for one minute 30 seconds. Let go of everything and scan the body from top to bottom.

Closing

Three, two, one. Fully inhale, hold for a moment and give the body one last gentle squeeze. Let go.

Take a few more deep breaths. Inhale into the tummy and chest, then release with a sigh. Return to your natural breathing through the nose, begin with slow movements and enjoy the refreshed energy. See you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Practical answers about the pattern, structure and how to approach the session.

How does each round end?

Every round includes a short inhale hold with gentle muscular engagement, followed by a longer and more relaxed exhale hold.

How long are the breath holds?

The inhale holds are 15, 20 and 30 seconds. The exhale holds are 30 seconds, one minute and one minute 30 seconds.

When could I use this session?

It was created as an energising morning practice or a focused reset before movement, but it should only be used in a safe resting position.
