Session overview

At a glance

This gentle practice uses a continuous six-second inhale and six-second exhale through the nose. The guided breathing begins at 00:42 and continues for just over five minutes before returning to a natural rhythm.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

A gentle, evenly paced practice for people who want a quieter alternative to fast breathing and long breath holds.

Pace

Slow and even

Six seconds in and six seconds out through the nose.

Intention

Settle and reconnect

For creating a simple point of focus when you want to slow down.

Experience

Accessible rhythm

No active hyperventilation or extended breath retention is used.

Guided session

Breathe with us

Sit or lie down somewhere safe and comfortable. Keep the breath within a manageable range and return to natural breathing whenever needed.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for education and general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, a cardiovascular condition or a serious respiratory condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to natural breathing and rest.

The intention

Consistency instead of intensity

The purpose of this session is not to push the breath, but to settle into a repeatable rhythm.

A six-second inhale followed by a six-second exhale creates a complete 12-second breathing cycle. The voice counts each phase so you do not have to watch a timer.

Keep the breath gentle and reduce the depth if a six-second count feels strained. A shorter, comfortable breath is more useful than forcing yourself to fill the entire count.

About the music

Written to follow the six-second cycle.

The original track uses a 60 BPM foundation and a 6/4 time signature, allowing the musical bar to match each six-second inhale and exhale. Handpan tones, waves and soft pads keep the arrangement spacious.

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today’s practice is coherent breathing, a gentle technique with a six-second inhale and a six-second exhale through your nose.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Practical answers about the pattern, structure and how to approach the session.

What is the breathing pattern?

Inhale through the nose for six seconds, then exhale through the nose for six seconds, with a smooth transition between them.

Are there breath holds in this session?

No extended breath holds are used. The practice follows one continuous inhale-and-exhale rhythm.

What if six seconds feels too long?

Shorten the count or return to natural breathing. The breath should remain smooth and comfortable rather than forced.
