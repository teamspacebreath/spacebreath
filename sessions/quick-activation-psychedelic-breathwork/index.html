Session overview

At a glance

This eight-minute active practice uses two rounds of 60 fast breaths at 87 BPM. Both rounds include a 30-second inhale hold, followed by an exhale hold of one minute in round one and one minute 15 seconds in round two.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

A short, high-energy option for people who are already comfortable with fast breathing and breath holds.

Energy

A quick activation

For moments when you want an active practice that fits into the day without becoming a longer journey.

Breathing style

Fast and continuous

For people who enjoy strong inhales, short releases and an immersive rhythm that carries directly into stillness.

Experience

Not a first fast-breath session

Best suited to people familiar with active breathwork. Choose a gentler session if fast breathing is new to you.

Guided session

Breathe with us

Sit or lie down somewhere safe and comfortable. This is an active practice, so keep the breath within a manageable range and stop whenever you need to.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This exercise is for educational and general wellbeing purposes only and is not medical advice. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have a history of epilepsy or cardiovascular issues. Never practise while driving, operating machinery, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Consult a qualified medical professional before trying a new breathing technique if you are unsure whether it is suitable for you.

Tingling, dizziness or emotional sensations may occur. Listen to your body, never force the breath and stop immediately if you feel uncomfortable or unwell.

The intention

A quick return to yourself

Short does not have to mean shallow. Eight focused minutes can create space to notice yourself differently.

This practice draws inspiration from psychedelic breathing styles, using a fast, continuous rhythm followed by stillness. The first round moves directly into the second, keeping the experience compact and immersive.

It is designed for moments when you want an active daily practice rather than a longer journey. Approach it with curiosity, remain aware of your body and choose a gentler breath or stop whenever that feels right.

Welcome and intention

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today’s session is inspired by psychedelic breathing. The difference is that it is much shorter.

We will only do two rounds, but the effect can still be quite strong. Think of it as an easy integration into your daily life to quiet the ego mind and get a little step closer to your true self.

The breathing itself sounds something like this. Focus on deep inhales. No full exhale, just a quick letting go.

All right. Lie down or sit upright, and we will begin in three, two, one.

Round one

Get into this fast breathing rhythm. Strong inhales, short exhales.

Five more from here. Now take a deep inhale and we hold here.

Squeeze your pelvic floor and your abdominals tight. Feel the energy rise all the way up to the top of your head.

Three, two, one. Exhale. We stay here for one minute.

Fully relax and just feel into the space around you.

Keep holding, and we will continue with our final round in three, two, one.

Round two

Go very deep here. Lift your chest with every inhale and bring this breath all the way up to your head.

The last five from here. And now, strong and deep inhale. Hold here on the inhale.

Gently engage your pelvic floor and your abdominals. Again, bring this energy up to the top of your head. And exhale.

Feel your body, feel the space, listen to the music and enjoy this last breath hold.

Three, two, one. Inhale. We hold here for a moment. Give your body a little squeeze if you like. And exhale.

Closing

Continue with your natural breathing through your nose. Give yourself some time here. Enjoy this moment, and do not forget to share this love with the world out there.

See you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Three direct answers about the psychedelic description, breathing pattern and recommended experience level.

Why is it called psychedelic breathwork?

The name describes the fast, immersive breathing style that inspired the practice. It does not involve any substances.

What is the breathing pattern?

There are two rounds of 60 fast breaths. Each finishes with a 30-second inhale hold, then an exhale hold of one minute or one minute 15 seconds.

Is this suitable for beginners?

This is one of the more active SpaceBreath sessions. If fast breathing is new to you, choose a gentler practice first and always follow the safety guidance.
