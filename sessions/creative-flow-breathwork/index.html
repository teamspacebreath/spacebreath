Session overview

At a glance

This 21-minute practice contains three rounds that move between slower and faster breathing. Every round finishes with a short inhale hold of up to 45 seconds.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

An active practice for people who want to gather their attention before creative work, move through a feeling of being stuck or explore breath and expression together.

Time

Twenty-one minutes

Three active rounds followed by quiet integration.

Intention

Creative focus

A reflective practice centred on expression and presence.

Experience

Moderate

Guided session

Breathe with us

Sit or lie down somewhere safe and comfortable. Keep the breath within a manageable range and return to natural breathing whenever needed.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for education and general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, a cardiovascular condition or a serious respiratory condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

Read the full safety guide →

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to natural breathing and rest.

The breathing pattern

Three rounds.
Breathe, hold, refocus.

Each round begins more slowly, rises in pace and finishes with a short inhale hold. Any engagement around the chest, neck and chin should remain gentle.

The intention

Expression as a yogic and creative focus

The guidance draws on Vishuddha, the throat chakra, as a symbolic framework for voice, expression and creative attention.

This yogic framework is presented as a reflective idea. The session does not claim to stimulate the thyroid medically or to increase creativity through a biological mechanism.

Lower the chin only gently and avoid pressure in the neck or throat. The inhale holds are optional and should end immediately if they become uncomfortable.

A reviewed transcript of the spoken guidance. Medical claims from the spontaneous introduction have been reframed as a yogic and reflective creative focus.

Open transcriptEnglish · 21 minutes

Welcome and technique

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today we are using the breath as a way to explore creative attention and expression.

The guidance refers to Vishuddha, the throat chakra, as a yogic symbol for voice and authentic expression. This is a reflective framework, not a medical claim about the thyroid or creativity.

We move through three rounds at different breathing speeds. Every round finishes with a short inhale hold, gently lowering the chin and lightly engaging the upper chest.

Round one

Begin slowly and deeply, filling the belly and then the chest. Let the exhale go softly and notice the movement of the body.

Increase the pace while staying controlled. At the end, inhale fully and hold comfortably. Lower the chin gently and use only light engagement around the upper chest, then release.

Rounds two and three

Round two returns to slow, deep breathing before increasing again. Stay aware of every inhale. Hold comfortably after the final inhale, then let all effort go.

In the final round, bring your full attention to the breath. Build the pace only as far as you can control it. Finish with a comfortable inhale hold, followed by an optional brief rest after the exhale.

Closing

Return gradually to natural breathing through the nose. Notice the room, the body and the quality of your attention.

Use this moment as a transition into creative work or simply as a conscious pause. Thank you for joining us. See you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Practical answers about the pattern, structure and how to approach the session.

What does the throat-chakra focus mean?

It is used as a yogic symbol for voice, expression and creative attention, not as a medical claim about the thyroid.

How long are the inhale holds?

They last up to about 45 seconds. End any hold sooner whenever your body needs air.

Can I skip the neck and chest engagement?

Yes. Comfort and safe breathing come first. Keep the head and neck neutral if that feels better.
