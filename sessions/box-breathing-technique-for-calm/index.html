Session overview

At a glance

This seven-minute practice follows the classic box breathing sequence: inhale for four seconds, hold for four, exhale for four and hold for four. The guided pattern begins at 00:44 and continues until the closing integration.

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

A structured calming practice for anyone who finds an even count easier to follow than faster breathwork or long breath holds.

Pattern

Four equal phases

Inhale, hold, exhale and hold for four seconds each.

Intention

Steadiness and calm

Useful before meditation or when you want a clear point of focus.

Experience

Simple and guided

Suitable for beginners who are comfortable with brief four-second holds.

Guided session

Breathe with us

Sit or lie down somewhere safe and comfortable. Keep the breath within a manageable range and return to natural breathing whenever needed.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for education and general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, a cardiovascular condition or a serious respiratory condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

Read the full safety guide →

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to natural breathing and rest.

The breathing pattern

Four equal phases.
A steady square.

Every complete cycle has four equal sides: inhale, hold, exhale and hold. Jonas counts each phase while the music keeps a 60 BPM foundation.

The intention

A clear structure for returning to the present

The equal count gives attention something simple and predictable to follow.

Box breathing divides each cycle into four equal phases. The shape is easy to remember, and the spoken count removes the need to watch the screen.

The holds are short, but they should still feel manageable. Shorten the count, skip a hold or return to normal breathing if you feel uncomfortable.

A meaningful transcript of the spoken introduction and closing. Repeated four-count guidance is described rather than duplicated cycle by cycle.

Open transcriptEnglish · 7 minutes

Introduction

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today we are diving into a short box breathing session. We will move through it at a relaxed four-second rhythm with each breath.

Box breathing is a beautiful way to calm your mind and settle into the present moment. I personally love using it before I meditate or whenever I feel nervous or overwhelmed. It is a gentle way to return to your breath.

Let’s get straight into it in three, two, one.

Guided pattern

Inhale for four seconds. Hold for four seconds. Exhale for four seconds. Hold after the exhale for four seconds.

Jonas repeats the four-count throughout the practice, guiding each side of the box in turn.

Closing

This is our last breath of today’s session. Now continue with your natural breath through your nose.

Thank you for being here. Enjoy the rest of your day, and see you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Practical answers about the pattern, structure and how to approach the session.

What is box breathing?

Box breathing uses four equal phases: inhale, hold, exhale and hold. This session uses four seconds for each phase.

How long does the practice last?

The complete video lasts seven minutes, with the guided box breathing beginning at 00:44.

What if the holds feel uncomfortable?

Shorten the count, leave out the hold or return to your natural breathing. Comfort and control are more important than completing every count.
