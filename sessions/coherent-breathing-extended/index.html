Session overview

At a glance

The extended version of our seven-minute coherent breathing session. The guided six-second rhythm begins at 00:57 and continues for ten minutes, giving the body time to slow down and the mind time to grow quieter before you return to natural breathing at 10:59. New to coherent breathing? Read our guide, What is coherent breathing?

Choosing your practice

Who this session is for

For anyone who has tried the seven-minute version and wants to stay in the rhythm longer, or who prefers a slow, steady practice to fast breathing and long breath holds.

Pace

Slow and even

Six seconds in and six seconds out through the nose.

Length

Time to settle

Around fifty full breaths, so the rhythm can really sink in.

Experience

Accessible rhythm

No active hyperventilation or extended breath retention is used.

Guided session

Breathe with us

Sit or lie down somewhere safe and comfortable. Keep the breath within a manageable range and return to natural breathing whenever needed.

Before you begin

Safety and contraindications

This session is for education and general wellbeing and is not medical advice. Do not practise while driving, in or near water, or anywhere a loss of consciousness could be dangerous. Do not practise if you are pregnant or have epilepsy, a cardiovascular condition or a serious respiratory condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional if you are unsure whether breathwork is suitable for you.

Read the full safety guide →

If you feel uncomfortable, dizzy or unwell, stop the practice, return to natural breathing and rest.

The intention

Give the rhythm time to land

Seven minutes is enough to find the rhythm. Fourteen gives it time to really settle in.

Each six-second inhale and six-second exhale makes one 12-second cycle, about five breaths a minute. Jonas counts every phase, so you never need to watch a timer.

The count is a guide, not a target. If six seconds feels strained, shorten it and follow what feels natural. The breath should stay soft and quiet, never an effort.

About the music

The same score,
given more room.

Harry extended the score from the seven-minute session to fourteen minutes. It is built at 60 BPM in 6/4, so one bar is one breath: six beats in, six beats out. Let the music count for you. The arrangement changes slowly, so the rhythm stays steady and the sound never pulls you out of it.

A meaningful transcript of the spoken introduction and closing. Repeated six-count guidance is described rather than duplicated line by line.

Introduction

Hi, and welcome to another session with SpaceBreath. Today’s journey is ten minutes of coherent breathing, a beautiful way to bring your whole body into sync, aligning the rhythm of your heart and your brain.

Simply sit or lie down comfortably and follow the breathing pattern: six seconds in and six seconds out. With every breath, try to relax a little more. Really let go of any tension in your body. There’s nothing to hold, nothing to do, just breathing. You might be surprised how naturally your mind follows.

All right, then please make yourself comfortable, and we will begin in three, two, one.

Guided breathing

Inhale through the nose for six seconds, then exhale through the nose for six seconds. Jonas counts each inhale and exhale from one to six throughout the practice.

Partway through: “Stay very present. Feel every inhale, how it enters. And every exhale, out it goes.”

Closing

This is our last breath of today’s journey. Now return to your natural breathing through your nose.

Thank you for sharing this space with us today. Enjoy the rest of your evening or your day, and see you soon.

Quick answers

Before you begin

Practical answers about the pattern, structure and how to approach the session.

How is this different from the 7-minute version?

Same pattern, same score, twice the time. The guided breathing runs for ten minutes instead of just over five, so the rhythm has longer to settle.

Are there breath holds in this session?

No extended breath holds are used. The practice follows one continuous inhale-and-exhale rhythm.

What if six seconds feels too long?

Shorten the count or return to natural breathing. If you are new to coherent breathing, the seven-minute session is a gentle place to start.
